# Before we connect anything: a gentle map of your Brain

This first conversation helps us understand what would make your Financial
Brain useful, what information belongs in it, and what must stay out. You do
not need to know technical terms or prepare twenty questions. We will take it
one question at a time, and you can pause, skip, or say "I do not know."

An unknown is useful. It becomes a visible gap for us to investigate together,
not something you are expected to guess.

---

## How to run this conversation

Paste this document into Claude Code and say:

> Interview me through this gently, one question at a time. Adapt the next
> question to what I just told you. Let me skip anything. Keep unknowns as
> unknown, and do not turn a possible person, business, account, or document
> mention into a confirmed fact.

Claude Code should explain why each question matters, wait for your answer, and
keep a short private summary on your computer. It must not ask you to paste a
password, token, authentication code, bank credential, private key, passkey
detail, or private file content into the conversation.

There is no fixed length. A short first pass is enough to begin safely. We can
return to the map as the evidence shows us what is missing.

---

## Part 1. What would make this useful?

Start here and ask only the follow-ups that apply.

**1.1** What would you most like your Financial Brain to help you understand?

**1.2** Is the picture mainly personal, household, business, or a combination?

**1.3** Who or what belongs in that picture?

This might include people, businesses, trusts, properties, or other entities.
Record each answer as owner-stated until documents or connected systems support
it. A name found in a document is only a possible mention until the owner
confirms what it represents.

**1.4** Which years or periods matter most right now?

**1.5** Who helps with the picture, such as a spouse, bookkeeper, accountant,
payroll provider, attorney, or financial adviser?

Do not contact anyone. This only records that another custodian or decision
maker may exist.

---

## Part 2. Where might the evidence live?

Ask about one source at a time. For each source, record:

- what the owner calls it;
- who controls the account or files;
- the part the owner is considering connecting;
- the approximate history the owner expects;
- whether it changes and, if so, how often;
- whether access requires another person or organization;
- whether the source is approved, excluded, undecided, or unavailable.

Possible source types include email, a file store, accounting software,
payroll, bank or investment feeds, tax files, meeting transcripts, messages,
calendar, a portal export, or paper records. This list is a reminder, not a
requirement. Do not ask the owner to connect every source.

Finding a source is not permission to connect or load it. The technician will
preview one valuable, low-risk source first and ask for approval before any
private data moves.

For important financial questions, gently check whether the current map covers
each applicable person or entity, the relevant years, accounts, books, payroll,
tax returns, filings, and payments. "Not applicable," "unknown," and
"unavailable" are different answers and must stay different.

---

## Part 3. What must stay out, and who may see what?

**3.1** Are any accounts, folders, mailboxes, topics, or custodians off limits?

**3.2** Is any material governed by someone else's confidentiality, such as
client files, employee records, health information, or legal material?

**3.3** Would a distinctive, non-secret word or phrase help us test that an
excluded area stayed out? The owner may skip this.

**3.4** Besides the owner, who may eventually ask the Brain questions, and what
whole sources should each person be allowed to use?

Financial Brain can have multiple access zones across its registered sources.
Each registered source can have at most one zone label, and a named access
grant can include one or more allowed zones. An unzoned source stays available
to the owner but is excluded from every named zone grant.

Zones are source-level boundaries. They do not separate individual files,
messages, or accounts inside one registered source. If one proposed source
mixes audiences, keep it owner-only until the technician can register safe
separate source scopes or the owner chooses a broader boundary. Never describe
a zone label by itself as proof that access is correct. The source assignment,
its derived document and search projections, and a real scoped search all need
to agree.

---

## Part 4. What might be hard to read or easy to miss?

Ask only what helps with the owner's current goal.

**4.1** What is the newest item that should be present, and roughly when was it
created or filed?

**4.2** What is the oldest item that still matters?

**4.3** Are there a few specific records the owner would be worried not to
find?

**4.4** What nicknames, project codes, or internal terms might mean something
different from their formal names?

**4.5** Which records might be scanned, photographed, faxed, or handwritten?

OCR exists for scanned PDFs, but it is off by default. It runs only after the
owner explicitly enables it because each scanned page uses Workers AI in the
owner's Cloudflare account. Before an OCR-enabled load, the technician shows
the estimated page count, cost range, time range, and daily spend cap. OCR text
is marked as machine-read and may be wrong; unreadable or low-quality results
are refused rather than treated as clean text.

A file that has not been inspected, produced no text, or failed extraction is
not automatically a scan. Its state remains unknown until the evidence proves
otherwise. Scan-only material also remains unreadable while OCR is off.

---

## Part 5. Questions are welcome, not homework

If a useful question comes to mind naturally, record it. One or two is enough,
and zero is also fine. The owner does not need to invent edge cases, plausible
events that never happened, or a fixed set before setup can continue.

Acceptance begins with the Brain's actual source receipts and evidence. Claude
Code proposes a small set of evidence-derived checks, explains why each one was
chosen, and asks the owner whether it matters. The owner can use it, reword it,
replace it with a real question, or skip it. No canned question becomes a fact,
and no question quota determines whether the owner is allowed to finish.

If the owner later wants a repeatable private regression suite, the Golden 20
evaluation is available as a strictly optional advanced exercise. It uses only
questions the owner chooses. It is never a prerequisite for install,
acceptance, handoff, Optimize, or support.

---

## What happens next

The technician turns this conversation into a private working map:

1. confirmed owner statements, possible mentions, and unknowns;
2. proposed sources, exclusions, and source-level access zones;
3. expected history, freshness, and important evidence;
4. likely OCR needs and the approval needed before any paid OCR run;
5. the smallest useful first source and one next action.

Nothing in this conversation authorizes a login, connection, upload, OCR run,
deployment, billing change, passkey ceremony, or access grant. Claude Code
should offer browser control for safe navigation on official pages and
non-secret fields, then return control before login, 2FA, secret or credential
entry, consent, billing, or a secure passkey window.

At every stage, the owner receives one clear question or action at a time. A
gap is not a failure. It is the next honest thing to solve together.
